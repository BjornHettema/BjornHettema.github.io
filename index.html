Ho Chi Minh City, 2025

Bins that tell the truck when they're full

Vollebak puts fill-level sensors inside public waste bins. I built the program that turns those readings into a collection route, so trucks stop driving past empty ones.

The road network is a weighted graph of intersections, bins and depot. Cached shortest paths and a nearest-insertion heuristic build the round, and when a bin crosses its threshold mid-route the route recalculates around it.

The harder question was which bins deserve a stop at all. I compared four ways of deciding, from simple thresholds to one that prices the risk of a bin overflowing rather than reading its current level.

Semarang, Jakarta and Surabaya, 2026

Four hundred year old canals, sinking cities

Java's colonial-era canals still carry the water, but the ground is dropping and the sea is rising. Over five months I worked out which stretches can still be saved, and what they could become.

I built a scoring method for individual canal segments and applied it identically in all three cities, so the cases could be compared rather than just described. That ran alongside historical document analysis, QGIS overlays of the original network against the current one, and photographic validation on site.

Sole author, named lead researcher by Universitas Diponegoro, graded 4.0. The study sits inside the Dutch City Deal "Timeless Canals" and was built to run in both directions, so what Java teaches goes back to the Dutch partners.

Deventer, 2026

A city measured at walking pace

Goudappel asked what a genuinely good pedestrian network for Deventer would look like. We built one, then went out and counted whether people actually used it that way.

I wrote the queries that pulled the base network out of OpenStreetMap and filtered it against explicit rules: alleys, cut-throughs, bridges and tunnels in, private land out. That became four network maps built to the Dutch national standard.

Intensities came from Goudappel's mobility panel, checked against our own counts at three locations. The two datasets did not agree, and what we concluded was that the hard numbers only make sense with street-level observation beside them.

Netherlands, 2025

A car park that could hold a rainstorm

An Erasmus+ programme with an international team. We turned a station-area parking site into a climate-resilient public space and presented the recommendations to Witteveen+Bos.

The engineering came first

I spent three years on electrical engineering and technical informatics before switching into spatial development. At the time it felt like starting over. It turned out to be the most useful thing I did, because the problems I care about now sit exactly between the two.

A routing algorithm and a zoning framework are both readable to me. That is a smaller club than it should be, and it means I can usually take a project from the policy question through to the thing that runs.

Fifteen months of that has been fieldwork in Southeast Asia, and it changed how I work. You cannot assess a canal from a desk in Deventer.
