Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam · 2025

Bins that tell the truck when they're full

Vollebak puts fill-level sensors inside public waste bins. I built the program that turns those readings into a collection route, so trucks stop driving past empty bins.

My role
Built the routing program
With
Hosoco, Ton Duc Thang University, Saxion

The road network is modelled as a weighted graph of intersections, bins and depot, with cached shortest paths and a nearest-insertion heuristic to build the round. When a bin crosses its threshold mid-route, the route recalculates around it.

The harder question was which bins deserve a stop at all. I compared four ways of deciding, from simple thresholds to one that prices the risk of a bin overflowing rather than reading its current level, then tested those against the three commercial routing services that could carry them.

  • Python
  • Google Routes API
  • Graph modelling
  • Heuristics
  • IoT sensors

Semarang, Jakarta & Surabaya, Indonesia · 2026

Four-hundred-year-old canals, sinking cities

Java's colonial-era canal systems still carry the water, but the ground is dropping and the sea is rising. I spent five months working out which stretches can still be saved, and what they could become.

My role
Sole author, named lead researcher
With
Saxion SAST and Universitas Diponegoro · graded 4.0

I built a scoring method for individual canal segments and applied it identically in all three cities, so the cases could actually be compared rather than just described. That was triangulated with historical document analysis, QGIS overlays of the original network against the current one, and photographic validation on site.

The study sits inside the Dutch City Deal "Timeless Canals" and was set up to run in both directions, so what Java teaches goes back to the Dutch partners as well.

  • QGIS
  • Comparative case study
  • Field survey
  • Governance mapping
  • Climate adaptation

Deventer, Netherlands · 2026

A city measured at walking pace

Goudappel asked what a genuinely good pedestrian network for Deventer would look like. We built one, then went out and counted whether people actually used it that way.

My role
Digital analysis and methodology
With
Client: Goudappel · Saxion StadsLAB

I wrote the queries that pulled the base network out of OpenStreetMap and filtered it against explicit rules: alleys, cut-throughs, bridges and tunnels in, private land out. That became four network maps built to the Dutch national standard.

Intensities came from Goudappel's mobility panel, checked against our own counts at three locations. The two datasets did not agree, and what we concluded was that the hard numbers only make sense with street-level observation sitting next to them.

  • OpenStreetMap
  • Overpass Turbo
  • QGIS
  • CROW standard
  • Survey design

Twente region, Netherlands · 2023 – 2025

Building on what the ground can take

A national programme asking municipalities to let soil and water decide where development goes, instead of the other way round. I helped turn that principle into a work plan they could use.

My role
Analysis and recommendations
With
Saxion and municipalities across Twente

Demographic analysis in R and GIS-based suitability mapping underpinned the recommendations, which came down to which areas can carry growth and which ones the subsurface quietly rules out.

About

The engineering came first

I spent three years on electrical engineering and technical informatics before switching into spatial development. At the time it felt like starting over. It turned out to be the most useful thing I did, because the problems I care about now sit exactly between the two.

A routing algorithm and a zoning framework are both readable to me. That is a smaller club than it should be, and it means I can usually take a project from the policy question all the way to the thing that runs.

Fifteen months of that has been fieldwork in Southeast Asia, and it changed how I work. You cannot assess a canal from a desk in Deventer. Both host universities published accounts of what I did there, which I am still quietly pleased about.
